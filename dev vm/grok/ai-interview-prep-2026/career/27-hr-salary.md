# 27 — Recruiter Screen, Offers, and Negotiation

**What this chapter is for:** so you treat the HR / recruiter loop as a scored round, not as “the easy call before the real interview.”

This is where people leak desperation, invent a current CTC, freeze on notice period, or quote a salary number they read in a WhatsApp group as if it were a 2026 fact.

**Do not invent compensation numbers in the room.** Public ranges move by city, company, level, and quarter. Use [levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi), the recruiter’s own band, offers you actually have, and local market conversations. Chapter 01’s US bands are **directional sanity checks**, not a script.

If you need a number in this chapter, it is labeled **illustrative** or **MARKET OBSERVATION**. It is not 2026 truth you should recite.

---

## 1. What the recruiter is actually doing

They are filling a packet:

| Field | Why it matters | If you fumble it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can you explain the job in one minute? | Filters tutorial candidates | No HM slot |
| Level they can defend | Band, interviewers, expectation | You get a loop you will fail or a band you will hate |
| Location / work auth / visa | Legal to hire | Instant stop |
| Notice / start date | Headcount timing | “Too slow” vs a later start that was always fine |
| Comp expectations | Whether a band exists | You get dropped *or* you self-lowball |
| Competing process | Their close plan | They ghost or they accelerate |
| Red flags | Lying, rigidity, “any AI job” | Notes that follow you into debrief |

They are not your coach. They can become an ally if you are precise. They will not rescue a chaotic story in calibration.

Recruiter screen length: 20–30 minutes. Prepare a 30s intro (Chapter 25), a 60s “what I shipped,” and 30s answers from §12. Stop talking.

---

## 2. When to talk money

| Stage | You | They |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application form | Put a range only if required. Prefer “negotiable / market for level.” | Often a filter. A fantasy number can auto-reject. |
| First recruiter call | Ask **level and band** before you name a demand. | They often ask first. See §5. |
| During technical loop | Do not reopen comp unless the level changed. | Ignore unless they do. |
| Verbal offer | Now you negotiate total comp, start date, scope. | They expect a conversation, not a grab. |
| Written offer | Confirm every component in writing. | “We’ll add it later” is not a component. |

**Principle:** information first, number second. “What level is this, and what is the band for that level in this location?” is a senior question. “I need 80 LPA” as sentence two is a gamble.

If they refuse to share a band, you may share a **range** tied to level and location, not a single point, and you may say you will calibrate after you understand scope.

## 3. Total compensation is not CTC is not in-hand

When they say “what are your expectations,” they may mean base, or CTC, or total. Ask which.

| Component | What it is | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Base / fixed** | Cash salary | Monthly vs annual; India: often “fixed CTC” ≠ in-hand |
| **Variable / bonus** | Annual or quarterly, sometimes “100% of target” | Target vs max; last year’s actual payout %; cliff |
| **Joining / sign-on** | One-time cash to bridge bonus/ESOP you forfeit | Clawback if you leave in 12 months; when it pays |
| **Relocation** | Move, visa, housing stipend | Taxable? Caps? Family? |
| **RSU / stock (public)** | Units × vest schedule | Grant value vs *refresh*; 4-year vest with 1-year cliff is common; tax |
| **ESOP (private)** | Options or shares in an unlisted company | Strike, pool, last 409A/valuation **if they share**, liquidity, buyback policy |
| **Retention / milestone** | Cash at 12/24 months | Written, not verbal |
| **Benefits** | Health, PF/401k, gratuity, meals, device | Material at some firms, rounding error at others |
| **On-call / shift / travel** | Stipend or time off in lieu | FDE travel days; AIOps pager |
| **Training / cert budget** | Rarely material | Do not trade base for “AI courses” |

**India MARKET OBSERVATION (structure, not a number):** product companies, GCCs, and startups in Bangalore / Hyderabad / Pune / Gurgaon / NCR often quote **CTC** (fixed + variable + PF + sometimes a notional ESOP line). Service-company offers may look large on CTC and thin on fixed. Always split **fixed vs variable vs stock**. An ESOP line inside CTC is not cash.

**US / EU MARKET OBSERVATION (structure):** total comp conversations are usually base + bonus + equity. Recruiter bands are often for a *level* in a *geo*. Remote US vs Bay Area vs London vs Berlin are different tables. Do not apply a Bay Area screenshot to Hyderabad.

**Illustrative band handling (say this, not a fake figure):**

> I do not want to invent a market number. I am targeting [level] in [city / remote]. I will look at your band, levels.fyi for this title and geo, and any competing written offers. If you can share the range for this level, I can tell you immediately whether we are in the same conversation.

If they press for a number before a band:

> For this scope I am looking at total compensation in line with [level] [role] in [geo]. I need the split — base, bonus, equity — to compare. If you need a placeholder, put me in the band you hire [level] [role] into; I will not be a point-offer from a blog.

Do not quote Chapter 01’s US ranges as *your* expectation unless you are actually in that market and that seniority. They are directional for US applied vs frontier ICs, and they are already stale the day after they were written.

## 4. Levels — get this right before you negotiate

A “senior AI engineer” at a 40-person startup is not L5 at a US lab and not “Lead” at an India GCC.

| You should ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| What is the **level name** (L4/L5, IC3/IC4, P4, Band, “Senior”, “Staff”)? | The loop and the band follow this. |
| Is this the same level as a non-AI SWE at that title? | Sometimes AI titles are inflated; sometimes they are one level higher in pay. |
| What does this level own in 6 months? | Scope mismatch is how you fail the loop *or* get bored. |
| Is the loop calibrated for switchers? | Chapter 26. If they need “shipped LLM prod SLO,” and you have a pilot, say so now. |

If they interview you as Staff and you are a mid-level switcher, you will fail architecture and then wonder about “HR.” Calibrate **down or sideways** if the honesty contract in Chapter 26 says you should. A mid-level offer that you can defend beats a Staff no.

**Script**

> I want to be hired at the level you can defend in calibration. I have [production proof from old job] and [honest AI scope]. If that maps to [Senior / L4 / IC3] rather than Staff, I would rather loop at that level than overclaim.

## 5. How to answer “current CTC” / “current comp”

They are anchoring. You do not have to donate a number that is irrelevant to this market.

**Legal / cultural note:** some US employers cannot or will not ask prior pay (jurisdiction-dependent). India, UAE, much of APAC, and many global capability centers **will** ask current CTC and expected CTC. Answer without theater and without padding.

### If you will share

> Current is [fixed] base, [variable] at target (last year paid [honest %]), [equity description if material]. That is [location] [old role] compensation, not a map of this job. I am evaluating this role on the band for [level] here.

Split the number. “My CTC is X” when X includes an ESOP notional and a 20% variable you never received is how you get a matching offer that is worse in cash.

### If the old number is low relative to this market

> I am under-market for this scope because [service-company band / non-AI title / smaller city]. I am not looking for a 10% bump on current. I am looking for this role’s band.

### If the old number is high

> I understand this geo / this company may sit differently. I care about level, scope, and total comp mix. If your band is below current cash I need to see equity, bonus, and growth; I will not waste a loop if we are far apart.

### If you will not share (more common in US)

> I prefer to work from your band for this level. I can confirm quickly whether it works.

If they insist and you need the loop: share a truthful range, not a padded one. Padding that they verify with an offer letter or a background check is a trust no.

**Do not:**

- Add a fake 30% because “everyone does”
- Quote expected CTC as current
- Convert ESOPs at a fantasy IPO
- Give a number in the wrong currency without saying so

## 6. Notice period, joining date, buyouts

**India MARKET OBSERVATION:** 30 / 60 / 90-day notice is common in product, GCC, and services. Some contracts say 90 even if the team “usually lets people go in 60.” Offer letters at the new company may say “join by DATE” with the band expiring.

| Topic | What to say | What not to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official notice | The number in your contract | “I can leave next week” if you cannot |
| Realistic last day | After you talk to your manager, not before an offer | Resigning on a recruiter screen |
| Buyout / recovery | Whether the new company has a policy; whether *you* can pay | Assuming they will buy 90 days |
| Garden leave / non-compete | Flag early if it is real | Hiding a non-compete until week two |
| Relieving letter / PF / background | You will complete a clean exit | “My manager will be angry so I cannot serve notice” as a negotiation ploy |
| Knowledge transfer | You will not strand a pager | Using “I am the only on-call” as a threat |

**Script**

> Contractual notice is [60] days. I will try to negotiate it down after I have a written offer, not before. I will not resign on a verbal. If you have a drop-dead start date, tell me now so we do not run a five-round loop we cannot close.

**US / EU:** two weeks is a norm, not a law. Senior roles and visas take longer. Relocation and background checks (especially if you handled production data) add weeks. Say the *true* constraint.

FDE / Solutions: ask whether start date includes a training ramp or a customer on-site in week two. That is a life constraint, not a money one.

## 7. Competing offers — use them without being a jerk

A competing offer is a **written** (or clearly verbal-from-compensable-recruiter) number at a real company. “I have interviews” is not an offer.

| Move | Signal |
| --- | --- |
| “I am in two other loops at similar level; their timeline is [date]. Can we complete by then?” | Professional. Helps them. |
| Share level + total + geo + components if you want them to match. | Useful. Be truthful. |
| “Match or I walk” on a verbal you cannot show | Cheap. They may. |
| Inventing a competing offer | Trust no if discovered. |
| Using a service-company CTC with notional ESOP as if it were FAANG TC | They will unpack it. |

**Script**

> I have a written offer from [type: product / GCC / lab] at [level], [geo]. Base [X], bonus [Y], equity [description]. I am not using it as a threat. I prefer this team because [specific]. If you can come to [band / component] by [date], I will accept. If you cannot, I will close the other loop.

Do not name the other company if your NDA or your taste says not to. Company *type* and *level* are enough.

If you have no competing offer, do not fake one. You can still negotiate on band, level, sign-on, and start date.

## 8. Negotiation process (not a fake number)

Negotiation is a sequence. Skip steps and you look unprepared or greedy.

### Step 1 — After verbal, before emotion

Write down:

```
Level:
Location / remote policy:
Base:
Bonus target and history:
Equity: units, type, vest, refresh *if stated*:
Sign-on / clawback:
Relocation:
On-call / travel expectation:
Start date:
Reporting line:
90-day success:
Open questions:
```

If a cell is empty, that is the first email, not “please add 20%.”

### Step 2 — Decide your walk-away *privately*

Walk-away is a **minimum acceptable package** given your runway, visa, notice, and the other offer — not a WhatsApp screenshot. If they meet it, you can say yes. If you cannot name it, you are negotiating by mood.

### Step 3 — One ask, ranked

Ask for the highest-leverage missing piece first:

1. **Level** (if the loop supports it) — this moves everything
2. **Base** — cash you can live on
3. **Sign-on** — bridges unvested equity / lost bonus
4. **Equity** — if you believe in the company and understand liquidity
5. **Start date / remote / title** — if money is already in band

Do not ask for all five as a shopping list. Pick one primary and one backup.

### Step 4 — The conversation

> Thank you. I want to join. The work on [evals / FDE / AIOps] is the job I want. On compensation: the band you quoted is [below / at the low end of] what I need to sign given [competing offer / geo / level / forfeited unvested]. I am asking for [specific component + amount or “top of the band”]. If we can do that, I will sign on [date]. I am not shopping this for sport.

Tone: calm, specific, time-boxed. No lectures on “AI talent shortage.” They have ten other loops.

### Step 5 — Get it in writing

Verbal “we’ll fix equity in six months” is not compensation. Refresh grants are discretionary unless written. Title changes “after probation” often do not happen.

### What not to do

| Move | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Negotiating before you would actually take the job | They spend political capital; you decline; you are marked |
| Renegotiating the day after you signed | Trust |
| Holding a exploding-offer hostage you created | They may withdraw |
| Threatening to fail background | Unhinged |
| Comparing to a cousin’s CTC in a different city | Irrelevant |
| Trading away HITL / ethics / on-call pay you will resent | You will leave in 8 months |

**Exploding offers:** ask for the decision date in writing. It is reasonable to request enough time to finish a loop already on the calendar. It is not reasonable to stall for three weeks “in case something better appears” after they have already stretched.

## 9. India vs global loops

| Topic | India-heavy loop (product / GCC / startup) | US / EU / remote-global |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter | Often agency + internal; CTC language | Internal recruiter; total comp language |
| Current pay | Usually asked | Sometimes illegal / discouraged |
| Notice | 30–90 days is a first-class issue | 2 weeks + visa/relocation |
| Variable | Often a large CTC fraction | Bonus % of base, clearer |
| Equity | ESOP illiquid; ask liquidity history | RSU more standard at public cos |
| Levels | Inconsistent titles | More mapped (but still messy) |
| Interviews | Can be 4–8 rounds; some take-homes | 4–6; on-site or virtual loop day |
| Agencies | Common; they may distort your CTC | Less so for these roles |
| Background | Address, PF, degree, last CTC | Employment, education, sometimes security clearance |

**MARKET OBSERVATION — Bangalore / Hyderabad / Pune / Gurgaon:** GCCs (US/EU product companies’ India engineering) often run a global-shaped loop with India HR mechanics (CTC, notice, PF). Startups may move faster and be vaguer on ESOP. Service companies / SIs hiring “GenAI consultants” may optimize for billing rate, not for evals — ask the Chapter 34 questions before you celebrate the title.

**Agencies:** they are paid on close. They may ask you to lower expectation to “fit the mandate,” or inflate your current CTC to raise their fee. You own the numbers that go on the form. If they submit a padded CTC, you live with the lie in background verification.

**Global remote:** confirm employment entity (US W2 vs India payroll vs contractor), equipment, data-residency (“you cannot have production logs on a laptop in [country]”), time overlap, and whether the *level* is the US level or a local one with a local band.

## 10. Visa, remote, and work authorization

State facts early. Surprises in round five waste everyone.

| Situation | Say on the first call |
| --- | --- |
| You need a visa / transfer | Country, visa type, who holds it, expiry, dependents |
| You are on a contract / vendor | You need a conversion; notice to the vendor |
| You can only work from [city] | Hybrid policy, office days, relocation yes/no |
| You need fully remote | Do not hide this until the offer |
| Travel % for FDE / Solutions | Your constraint (caregiving, passport, willingness) |
| Export control / citizenship | If the JD mentions it, ask; do not guess |

**Script**

> I am authorized to work in [country] as [citizen / visa]. I [do / do not] need sponsorship. I can be in [office] [N] days. I cannot do 50% customer travel; I can do [N] days a quarter. If that kills the role, we should stop now.

Do not apologize for legality. Do not hide a constraint the job requires.

## 11. Red flags (HR and recruiter)

Walk or slow down if you see several of these. One messy recruiter is not a reason to drop a good team.

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Will not name level or band after you are invested | You cannot decide |
| JD is “AI Engineer” with every buzzword | You will get a random loop (Chapter 02) |
| “We need autonomous agents in prod in 30 days” as a non-engineer mandate | You will be the incident |
| Recruiter wants you to lie about notice or CTC | They will not protect you in BGV |
| Unpaid “trial project” that is their customer deliverable | Not an interview |
| Offer letter missing equity details they sold verbally | Believe the PDF |
| Title Senior, scope junior, pay junior | You will fail or stall |
| “We cannot tell you the product” *and* cannot tell you the constraint | You cannot prep; sometimes real, often chaos |
| Pressure to resign before written offer | Never |
| Different band from two recruiters at the same company | Demand a single owner |
| Quota / OTE for a role they called “pure IC” | Solutions vs Engineer (Chapter 34) |
| “AI is a center of excellence; no production owner” | You will write decks |

Positive signals: they ask about evals, on-call, HITL, and whether you have *not* shipped LLM prod — and they still want you. That is a team that read the same honesty contract.

## 12. Recruiter question bank (30-second answers)

Fill brackets. Time them. If an answer exceeds 45 seconds, cut.

### 12.1 Tell me about yourself

Use the 30s Chapter 25 template for the role. End with why *this* job. No childhood, no course list.

### 12.2 Why are you looking? Why leaving?

> I am a [role] on [domain]. I want my next job to own [AI system type] in production, including [evals / HITL / landing]. I have already started that shift with [pilot]. I am not fleeing a PIP and I am not trashing [employer]. I am choosing this problem class.

### 12.3 Why this company?

One non-homepage fact (product constraint, on-prem, FDE, regulated). If you do not have one, say what the JD implies and that you will ask a sharp question (Chapter 34). Do not invent a blog post you did not read.

### 12.4 What are you looking for in your next role?

> A team that treats evals and [ACL / on-call / customer v0] as engineering. A level you can defend. Work I can describe without lying about production.

### 12.5 What is your current title / level?

Truth. Then the target. Do not relabel Support Engineer as AI Engineer on this call.

### 12.6 Have you shipped LLMs to production?

Use Chapter 26 §7.4. Recruiter notes will be quoted later. This is the worst place to overclaim.

### 12.7 What is your tech stack?

> Production: [Python, SQL, cloud, on-call tools you actually used]. AI: [one retrieval stack, one eval approach, one orchestrator if any]. I can go deep on those. I will not list eight agent frameworks.

### 12.8 Coding — are you comfortable with a practical Python round?

> Yes. I expect JSON, retries, tests, not puzzle-hard LeetCode unless you say so. If this loop is Staff-architect with no coding, tell me; I still write code.

If you are not comfortable, do not take a GenAI IC loop this month. Study Chapter 06.

### 12.9 Current compensation?

§5. Split components. Currency. Geo.

### 12.10 Expected compensation?

> I want the band for [level] [role] in [geo]. Please share it. I will tell you immediately if we are aligned. I will not invent a market number on this call.

If they legally need a number on a form: a **range** you would actually accept, not a fantasy.

### 12.11 Notice period / when can you start?

§6. Contractual number. You will not resign on a verbal.

### 12.12 Are you interviewing elsewhere?

> Yes, a small number of [role]-shaped loops. I am not spraying 80 applications. I can share timelines so we are fair to each other. I do not have a written competing offer [or: I do, timeline is X].

### 12.13 Work authorization / location / remote?

§10. Facts.

### 12.14 Are you open to [FDE travel / on-call / hybrid 3 days]?

Yes, no, or a number. Do not say yes to 50% travel and negotiate it after the offer as if you had never heard.

### 12.15 This role is [Solutions with quota / IC]. Are you sure?

> I applied because [JD verbs]. If there is quota / on-site / pager, I need that explicit. I am [willing / not willing]. I do not want to discover in round four that this is a sales role.

### 12.16 Why should we hire a switcher?

> Because you need [production habit] on [AI system], and I have the first in production and the second at honest pilot scope with evals. You should not hire me if you need a researcher or someone who has already paged on a 50-QPS assistant.

### 12.17 What are your salary expectations in USD / INR / EUR?

Do not convert sloppily. Ask which currency the role pays. Do not quote a US TC screenshot for an India payroll role.

> The role is paid in [currency] on [entity]. I will talk in that currency. Please share the band.

### 12.18 We need an answer by Friday (exploding)

> I can give you a decision by [reasonable date]. I have [loop / family / visa] already scheduled. I want this to be a yes I can keep, not a panic yes.

### 12.19 Can you do an unpaid assignment this weekend?

> I can do a time-boxed take-home that is clearly an interview. I cannot deliver customer work for free. If it is eight hours, I need that up front.

### 12.20 Anything else we should know?

Only real constraints: competing deadline, notice, visa, “I have not shipped LLM prod,” “I need to loop at Senior not Staff.” Do not confess irrelevant anxiety.

## 13. Offer checklist (before you sign)

Print this. If a line is blank, ask.

- [ ] Legal entity and location of employment
- [ ] Level and title (internal and external)
- [ ] Base, pay frequency, currency
- [ ] Bonus: target, max, last year’s payout culture if they will say
- [ ] Equity: type, units or value, vest, cliff, refresh *policy vs promise*
- [ ] Sign-on: amount, pay date, clawback
- [ ] Relocation / visa
- [ ] Notice they require if you leave; probation
- [ ] On-call, travel %, quota / OTE
- [ ] Background verification: what they check (including last CTC in India)
- [ ] Non-compete / IP / moonlighting
- [ ] Start date you can actually hit
- [ ] Manager name and team
- [ ] 90-day success in one paragraph (email is enough)

If equity is ESOP: strike price, number of options, current 409A/internal valuation **if shared**, what happened in the last liquidity event, and whether the offer’s “CTC includes ESOP” line is cash. If they will not explain, price ESOP at **zero cash** in your personal walk-away math. That is not cynicism. That is arithmetic.

## 14. Fail modes

| Fail | What it sounds like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fake market expert** | “Everyone in Bangalore gets X LPA for GenAI” | Ask their band; use levels.fyi; no WhatsApp facts |
| **CTC theater** | One big number, no split | Fixed / variable / stock |
| **Overclaim on the HR call** | Recruiter writes “prod multi-agent” | Chapter 26 scripts |
| **Resigning on verbal** | “I already put in notice” | Written offer first |
| **Shopping list negotiation** | Base + sign-on + title + remote + level | One primary ask |
| **Agency-owned numbers** | Recruiter padded your CTC | You own the form |
| **Wrong currency** | US TC for India payroll | Entity first |
| **Ignoring quota/travel** | Surprise FDE life | Ask Chapter 34 questions *before* money |
| **Desperation** | “I will take anything” | You still have a walk-away |
| **Arrogance** | “AI talent is impossible to hire, so…” | They will test that in the loop |

## 15. Drill

1. Write your current comp **split** on paper. If you cannot, you are not ready to talk CTC.
2. Write walk-away and ideal **as ranges in the currency of the role**, after looking at levels.fyi / recruiter band / local conversations. Label them personal, not “market facts.”
3. Record answers 12.1, 12.6, 12.9, 12.10, 12.11. Each under 35 seconds.
4. Role-play a recruiter who refuses to give a band. Practice asking twice, then giving a range, then stopping.
5. If you are a switcher, glue this chapter to Chapter 26: the recruiter packet should say **honest scope**, not a title you wish you had.

You are ready when a recruiter can write: level, geo, notice, work auth, honest production story, and a compensation conversation that does not contain a made-up market statistic. That packet is what gets you the loop. The loop is still earned in Chapters 23–25 and the technical guides — HR cannot fake it for you.
